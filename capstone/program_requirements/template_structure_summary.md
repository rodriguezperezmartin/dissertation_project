# HSOG Capstone Template — Structured Manuscript Summary

**Source:** [HSOG_Capstone_Template_Fall2024.docx](HSOG_Capstone_Template_Fall2024.docx), "Capstone Project Template."

## Formatting Rules
- 1-inch margins all sides; Times New Roman 12-pt; double-spaced; left-justified body text; two spaces after punctuation; Arabic page numbers upper-right (suppressed on page 1); current APA style manual.
- Title page: ALL CAPS title; "A Capstone Project Presented in Partial Fulfillment of the Requirements for the Degree Doctor of ____"; approved-by line for Committee Chair.

## Manuscript Structure (Front Matter → 5 Chapters → References/Appendices)
- Acknowledgments (optional)
- Dedication (optional)
- **Executive Summary** — max 1 page; no statistics/references/direct quotes; must open with: *"The purpose of this project is to (improve or solve) the problem of ____ and to design ____ to address the problem."*

### Chapter One: Introduction to the Project and Related Literature — **minimum 25 pages**
- **Overview** — purpose, practical problem, research question, chapter roadmap. Do NOT answer the research question or propose a solution here.
- **The Setting and Background** — the specific public-policy/administration/criminal-justice/national-security setting; must correspond to the chosen doctoral program; describe actors, site, stakeholders in detail.
- **Problem Statement & Significance** — state "The problem is…" clearly; must be backed by non-anecdotal evidence (reports, peer-reviewed studies); identify the organization/population/stakeholders impacted. Do NOT propose a solution yet.
- **Purpose Statement** — must begin: *"The purpose of this project is to improve or solve the problem of…."* Flows directly from the problem statement.
- **Research Question(s) and Dependent Variable:**
  - Capstone research questions are **more accommodating of descriptive/pragmatic questions** than dissertation questions (which must be explanatory).
  - Well-formed capstone RQ: (a) stated as a question, (b) specific/restricted in scope, (c) testable, (d) replicable, (e) no ethical/moral implementation problem.
  - Typical capstone: **1–3 research questions.**
  - **Key structural difference from a dissertation:** a capstone's dependent variable only needs to cover a **chosen slice of the variation spectrum** (e.g., only high-compliance cases), not the full range low-to-high that a dissertation must explain.
- **Summary & Roadmap** — conclude chapter, preview what follows.

### Chapter Two: Literature Review — **often the longest chapter; "30 pages or more"**
- Reviews **500+ peer-reviewed sources** in the research process; **minimum 40 sources must be integrated into the written chapter.**
- Not for background/practical-importance info (that's Chapter 1) — this chapter maps the theoretical landscape: who are the key scholars, what did they conclude, *how* did they get there, were their methods sound.
- Must identify a theoretical framework and justify its application. Example frameworks suggested for **public policy**: Downs' (1957) Rational Choice Theory, Olson's (1965) Collective Action Theory, Arrow's (1951)/Black's (1958) Public Choice Theory, Elite Theory, Group Theory, Systems Theory, Institutional Theory, Lindblom's Incremental Theory.
- Must build justification for the Chapter 3 research design choices.

### Chapter Three: Research Design and Procedures
- **Research Question(s) and Hypothesis(es)** — full explanations + formal hypotheses (one per RQ typically) + null hypotheses. Hypothesis criteria: one sentence, states expected relationship, testable, concise, grounded in prior research.
- **Research Design** — must be specific (e.g., not just "quasi-experimental" but "quasi-experimental static-group comparison design"), with full rationale/justification grounded in methodological literature, detailed enough for replication.
- **Variables & Measurement** — conceptualize + operationalize + measure every variable (IV, DV, controls), grounded in the literature review.
- **Unit of Analysis, Participants, and Sample** — justify population/sample/size; data gathering to the point of saturation; discuss (likely limited) generalizability and why broader-generalizability approaches were rejected if applicable.
- **Data Sources and Collection** — detailed description of data sources/instruments/collection procedures and sequencing rationale. **See [handbook_summary.md](handbook_summary.md) §5 for the mandatory quantitative+qualitative+interview requirements.**
- **Data Analysis** — technique must match RQs/hypotheses/data type; full detail (e.g., assumption tests, effect size statistic, alpha; or coding schemes for qualitative document analysis).
- **Validity, Ethical Considerations and Biases** — validity discussion, ethical procedures (confidentiality, pseudonyms), researcher's own relationship/bias to the issue/site, and mitigation strategies.
- **Permissions (if applicable)** — site/gatekeeper permission wording; IRB generally not required (see handbook_summary.md §6) unless publishing/disseminating beyond the site, or site/DoDEA requires it.
- **Summary**

### Chapter Four: Findings
- Present findings in a form appropriate to the research design (e.g., organized by hypothesis, or as narrative case studies).
- Conclude by identifying and summarizing themes. **No analysis/interpretation/discussion here** — that's Chapter 5.

### Chapter Five: Conclusions
- Analysis, interpretation, discussion of results; address internal/external validity threats and generalizability; address alternative explanations for findings.
- **Proposed Solution to Practical Problem** — a fully detailed, implementable solution (not just named — e.g., if "three professional development sessions," each session must be described in full, including format/content/timeline).
- **Solution Implications** — positive/negative implications across resources, funding, roles, timeline; stakeholder-specific recommendations; limits of the proposed solution.
- **Policy Implications (as appropriate)**
- **Evaluation Plan** — concrete plan (goal-based/outcomes-based/formative/summative) to assess the solution's effectiveness post-implementation: who's responsible, how conducted, what criteria.
- **Theory Application & Implications (as appropriate)** — how the study applies/contributes to existing academic theory; recommendations for further research.
- **Summary** — reiterate the 1–2 most important takeaways.

### Back Matter
- References
- Appendix/Appendices (all procedural materials: site permission letters, consent forms, interview protocols, survey instruments, etc.)

## Key Takeaways vs. a Traditional Dissertation
1. **Narrower, bounded dependent variable** is acceptable (a slice of the variation spectrum, not the full range).
2. **Descriptive/pragmatic research questions are acceptable**, not just explanatory ones.
3. **Primary data collection (interviews + at least one other method) is the norm**, not optional — a purely secondary-data/published-literature analysis does not fit this template's Chapter 3 requirements as written.
4. **The manuscript must culminate in an actionable, implementable solution** (Chapter 5) plus an evaluation plan — not just findings/discussion.
5. **Minimum length benchmarks:** Ch. 1 ≥ 25 pages; Ch. 2 ≥ 30 pages with ≥ 40 integrated sources.
