# HSOG Scholarly Project Handbook — Structured Summary

**Source:** [HSOG_Scholarly_Project_Handbook_2026.pdf](HSOG_Scholarly_Project_Handbook_2026.pdf), effective August 24, 2026.

## 1. Dissertation vs. Doctoral Project (Critical Distinction, Verbatim Definitions)

> Programs which grant the Doctor of Philosophy (Ph.D.) degree require students to write a **dissertation**. A dissertation is a work of original research designed and conducted by a doctoral student that contributes to the body of knowledge in a particular academic field. A dissertation should explore a problem that is documented in the academic literature of the field. It should be grounded in, and contribute to, an established theoretical tradition in the field. It should be explanatory in nature, rather than simply exploratory. It should produce findings that are **generalizable**.

> Programs which grant doctoral degrees other than the Ph.D. (D.P.A., D.C.J., **D.P.P.**, D.L.P.) require students to write a **doctoral project**. A doctoral project is a work of original research designed and conducted by a doctoral student that brings academic research to bear on a **practical problem**. A doctoral project should draw from academic work to illuminate a problem of practice at a **specific site or in a specific context**.

> For this handbook, a doctoral project is a **site-specific, evidence-based initiative** aimed at addressing a problem or improving outcomes within a particular setting. Unlike traditional research, doctoral projects are **not generalizable**; they focus on applying knowledge to solve practical issues in a specific context. Examples include capstone projects, applied research, and quality improvement initiatives.

**Bottom line:** the D.P.P. capstone is explicitly NOT required (or expected) to be generalizable. It must be tied to a specific organization/site with a practical problem and real stakeholders who can use the findings.

## 3. Faculty Support Structure
- **Project Chair:** primary advisor; guides research process start to finish; reviews chapters, gives feedback, oversees progress from proposal to final defense.
- **Methodologist:** external oversight role (not on the committee); ensures research design/data collection/analysis methods meet rigorous academic standards; verifies institutional and field-specific research guidelines.
- **Selection process:** while completing the Research Plan Review form, review the HSOG faculty roster (in Doctoral Community), identify faculty whose expertise aligns with the intended research area, list preferences on the form; department matches based on availability.
- **Appeals:** written request to Dean of College/School → Associate Dean of Graduate School → Dean of Graduate School (final).

## 5. Data Collection Requirements (Appendix C, Task 3 — Chapter 3 Methods)
- **At least one quantitative AND at least one qualitative data collection approach required.**
- **Interviews are required** as one of the data collection approaches, unless a formal, research-based justification for exemption is submitted to the chair and approved by the Research Director. **Even if exempted from interviews, 3 total data collection approaches are still required.**
- **Interview method:** minimum 5, maximum 10 participants; protocol should typically include ≥10 open-ended questions; pseudonyms required for confidentiality; AI may be used to transcribe recordings but transcriptions must be manually verified against the recording.
- **Survey method:** minimum 15 participants; minimum 10 topic-focused prompts (excluding demographics).

## 6. Site Permission & IRB (Appendices I & J)
- Doctoral projects are typically conducted **within a specific organization**; official site authorization/permission letter required from an authorized official **before any data collection** (recruitment, surveys, interviews).
- Securing site permission is the candidate's sole responsibility; Liberty cannot override a site's denial.
- If the approved research proposal does NOT require a specific site/organization, the site-permission requirement **may be waived** — the chair requests this waiver documentation via HSOGDoctoralPrograms@liberty.edu.
- **IRB is typically NOT required** for doctoral projects because they are not "generalizable" per federal regulations (45 CFR 46.102(e)(1)) — exceptions: (a) projects at DoD Education Activity (DoDEA) schools, (b) if the site itself requires documented IRB review before granting permission, (c) if the candidate intends to independently publish/disseminate the project beyond the site (requires LU IRB approval **before** data collection, plus CITI training).

## 7. Proposal Defense & Final Defense (Appendices G & P)
- Both defenses: facilitated by the chair via Microsoft Teams, recorded, ~1 hour, candidate must use webcam, have reliable internet/phone, professional dress, professional location, tech support on hand.
- Attendance limited to: candidate, chair, invited LU faculty, invited LU doctoral students.
- Must be officially scheduled through the HSOG doctoral support team to count.
- **Proposal Defense** = formal evaluation of the approved Chapters 1–3 manuscript + readiness to proceed to data collection.
- **Final Defense** = formal evaluation of the approved Chapters 1–5 manuscript, the completed study, and readiness for degree conferral.
