## Working Title (Draft)
Healthcare Digital Infrastructure as a Driver of Public Policy Outcomes in Emerging Economies

## Research Question (Current Leading Question — as of 2026-09-14)
To what extent does the implementation of predictive AI models and interoperable digital health architectures reduce reactive crisis spending in public health interventions within emerging economies? ("Leapfrog Advantage & AI Integration" angle — see [research_questions.md](research_questions.md) for the full evolution log and original broader framing.)

## Core Thesis
Health should be framed not merely as a medical/humanitarian issue but as a driver of human capital and economic growth. Emerging economies have a structural advantage over developed nations: the absence of legacy health IT systems allows them to build agile, interoperable digital health infrastructure from the ground up. The central gap this capstone targets is the near-total absence of built-in monitoring/evaluation systems in health policy interventions — reporting is treated as an afterthought rather than designed in from the start, which prevents timely, evidence-based policy correction.

**Refinement (as of PLCY 700, Fall 2026):** The shift from reactive to proactive spending is not purely a technology-adoption question — it is conditional on *which policy instrument* drives DPI adoption (a legal mandate/"stick" vs. a donor-funded roadmap/"carrot", per Stone's inducements framework) *and* whether that instrument is paired with sufficient capability investment (funding, connectivity, training, standards). A mandate without capability investment, or a roadmap without enforcement durability, both risk failing to produce the reactive-to-proactive shift. The thesis also now explicitly carries an **equity dimension**, not just efficiency: predictive AI/interoperable infrastructure is an efficiency play, but if deployed first in wealthier or urban regions, it risks widening — not closing — existing health access gaps. See [Theoretical Framework](#theoretical-framework-informed-by-plcy-700) below and [case_studies/policy_instruments_carrot_stick_framework.md](case_studies/policy_instruments_carrot_stick_framework.md) for the full argument.

## Theoretical Framework (Informed by PLCY 700)
**This section is a living inventory, not a finished chapter.** It will keep growing as later coursework (PLCY 805, 809, 810, 811, 812, 885) and professor feedback add new theoretical tools — treat it as the running backbone for the capstone's eventual Theoretical Framework chapter, not a conclusion.

- **Causal theory structure** (Birkland Ch. 9 & 11; Stone's causal stories) — states the capstone's argument formally as cause (M&E treated as a policy afterthought) → intervention (predictive AI + interoperable digital health architecture, designed in from the start) → effect (a measurable shift from reactive to proactive spending). See [coursework/PLCY700/week07/assignments/presentation_roadmap.md](../coursework/PLCY700/week07/assignments/presentation_roadmap.md).
- **Policy instrument typology — carrots vs. sticks** (Stone Ch. 11–12, Inducements) — classifies real-world DPI-fostering policies (Mexico's legal mandate vs. Guatemala's donor-funded roadmap) and scores them against Stone's 4 inducement-success prerequisites: Awareness, Value Alignment, Capability, Credibility. See [case_studies/policy_instruments_carrot_stick_framework.md](case_studies/policy_instruments_carrot_stick_framework.md).
- **Efficiency vs. equity tension** (Stone Ch. 2–3) — requires evaluating any DPI intervention on both dimensions, not efficiency alone; named explicitly in the Week 7 oral presentation and Week 7 quiz (Question 11).
- **Institutional resistance mechanics** (Wilson, 1989, *Bureaucracy*) — explains *why* government health agencies resist adopting new reporting technology (turf protection, organizational culture, compliance-type observability: production/procedural/craft/coping agencies) independent of the technology's merit. See [coursework/PLCY700/week06/assignments/book_notes_wilson_bureaucracy.md](../coursework/PLCY700/week06/assignments/book_notes_wilson_bureaucracy.md).
- **Policy process models** (Birkland Ch. 9–11: Stages Heuristic, Multiple Streams Framework, Punctuated Equilibrium Theory, Advocacy Coalition Framework, Institutional Analysis and Development, Social Construction of Target Populations) — candidate models for explaining *how* a DPI mandate gets adopted, diffused, or stalled across countries; which model (or combination) best fits cross-country DPI diffusion is still an open question (see below). See [coursework/PLCY700/week08/assignments/reading_chapter_notes.md](../coursework/PLCY700/week08/assignments/reading_chapter_notes.md).
- **Rights, power, and facts as political instruments** (Stone Ch. 14–16) — who controls patient health data, who is empowered or disempowered by how M&E metrics are designed and measured, and how data-governance rights are mobilized (or denied) in the polis. Directly deepens the "M&E as afterthought" argument: measurement itself is a political act, not a neutral technical one. See [coursework/PLCY700/week08/assignments/science_and_theory_homework.md](../coursework/PLCY700/week08/assignments/science_and_theory_homework.md).
- **Epistemological foundation** (Walliman; Popper/Kuhn/Feyerabend) — grounds why the capstone's research question must be falsifiable and empirically testable, and situates the methodology within a positivist/empirical approach rather than a purely interpretivist one. See [coursework/PLCY700/week02/assignments/short_paper_scientific_inquiry.md](../coursework/PLCY700/week02/assignments/short_paper_scientific_inquiry.md).

## Capstone Methodology Approach (Revised 2026-10-17 per Official HSOG Handbook/Template)
**This section supersedes the prior "existing empirical data only" framing.** Having read the official [HSOG Scholarly Project Handbook](program_requirements/handbook_summary.md) and [Capstone Template](program_requirements/template_structure_summary.md), the D.P.P. capstone is explicitly defined as a **site-specific, non-generalizable, practitioner-facing doctoral project** — not a dissertation-style analysis of published/secondary data alone. This changes the required evidence base:

- **The Problem (Why):** Fragmented health data in a specific health system leads to reactive crisis spending and hinders human capital development — framed for one real site, not "emerging economies" broadly.
- **The Site (Required, Not Optional):** The project must be anchored to one specific organization/governmental context that can formally grant site permission and use the findings. See [Site & Organizational Partner](#site--organizational-partner-new-2026-10-17) below.
- **The Methodology (How the degree is earned):** The handbook requires **primary data collection** — at minimum one quantitative *and* one qualitative method, with **interviews mandatory** (5–10 participants) unless a formal exemption is approved (and even then, 3 total methods are still required). Existing published/empirical data (policy documents, economic datasets, case-study literature already gathered in [case_studies/](case_studies/)) remains essential for Chapters 1–2 context and theoretical grounding, but **cannot by itself satisfy Chapter 3's methodology requirements**.
- **The Technical Build (Still a Differentiator, Not a Substitute):** The ~20 years of AI/MLOps background can still inform the *solution* proposed in Chapter 5 (e.g., a predictive-model or interoperability-architecture recommendation) and may inform data analysis tooling, but it does not replace the primary data collection requirement.
- **The Analysis (The Doctorate):** Earned by collecting and analyzing real data from the chosen site (interviews with practitioners + at least one additional method) to answer the research question, then proposing a detailed, implementable solution (Chapter 5) with an evaluation plan — matching the Template's required structure, not a purely econometric/secondary-data analysis.

**Bottom line:** this is now a field-research project requiring a real site, real practitioner interviews, and an implementable solution — not a desk-research policy analysis with an optional PoC.
