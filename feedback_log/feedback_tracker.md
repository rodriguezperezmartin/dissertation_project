## Evaluation Criteria Observed
_(Document explicit or implicit rubrics/criteria faculty use to grade assignments and, eventually, the dissertation proposal/defense)_

- **PLCY 700, Week 4 — Literature Assessment and Research Question rubric (Dr. Boykin):** graded on 7 criteria — (1) identification of 3+ common elements/themes across literature, with evaluation of significance; (2) identification of 3+ gaps/weaknesses, with evaluation of significance; (3) 3+ implications for future research (key texts, theories, methodologies, trends); (4) a scientific research question that is testable, falsifiable, phrased as a question, and follows naturally from the literature; (5) accurate/consistent APA formatting; (6) clear writing mechanics within a strict word count (2,000–2,500 words); (7) logical organization. Pattern: faculty consistently reward the number **3** as the threshold for "advanced" on content criteria (3+ themes, 3+ gaps, 3+ implications) — a useful heuristic for future assignments and for the dissertation literature review chapter.
- **PLCY 700, Week 6 — Book Review Assignment rubric (Dr. Boykin, 175 pts):** graded on 7 criteria — (1) Central Thesis and Book Choice (30 pts); (2) Evaluation of book's main themes and methods, incl. explicit qualitative/quantitative identification (40 pts, the heaviest-weighted single criterion); (3) Evaluation of book's conclusions, incl. soundness and weaknesses/oversights (30 pts); (4) Evaluation of the book's place within the public policy field, requiring specific citations from course readings (23 pts); (5) APA formatting (17 pts); (6) Writing mechanics within the 1,500–2,000 word range (18 pts); (7) Organization (17 pts). Pattern: methodological analysis (qualitative vs. quantitative + research design explanation) is the single most heavily weighted content criterion — worth prioritizing depth there. Full rubric: [week06/learning_objectives.md](../coursework/PLCY700/week06/learning_objectives.md).
- **PLCY 700, Week 2 — Short Paper: Scientific Inquiry and Research Questions rubric (Dr. Boykin, 125 pts):** graded on 6 criteria — (1) Article Choice, 1 academic + 1 popular press, same policy area (13 pts); (2) Compare/Contrast using Popper/Kuhn/Feyerabend + social research approach (25 pts, tied for heaviest weight); (3) Research Questions & Explanation (25 pts, tied for heaviest weight); (4) Scientific Method and Biblical Worldview (25 pts, tied for heaviest weight); (5) APA formatting (12 pts); (6) Writing mechanics within 1,250–1,750 words (13 pts); (7) Organization (12 pts). Pattern: three content criteria are equally weighted at 25 pts each — article analysis, research questions, and biblical worldview integration all matter equally, unlike Week 4/6 where one criterion dominates. Full rubric: [week02/learning_objectives.md](../coursework/PLCY700/week02/learning_objectives.md).
