# Dr. Jason Ross — "Why Are You Here?" (Required Viewing, Final Week)

**Speaker:** Dr. Jason Ross, Associate Dean and Associate Professor, Helms School of Government, Liberty University
**Context:** Required viewing for the final week of PLCY 700, addressed to all doctoral students across the Helms School's programs (criminal justice, public administration, public policy).

## Key Takeaways

1. **Count the cost before and during the program.** A doctoral program is a multi-year commitment of time, money, and emotional/family bandwidth — think clearly about what you hope to accomplish, and revisit that question as you go, not just at entry.
2. **It can be a lonely road.** You are doing research nobody else has done. Faculty teach, advise, and give feedback, but the capstone/capstone project is ultimately **yours** — choose your topic wisely because you own it.
3. **You cannot be passive.** Earlier degrees train you to be responsive (faculty assigns, you respond). In the research phase, faculty will not tell you what to research — they can only respond to what you put in front of them. If you don't know what you're researching, you won't get research done.
4. **Narrow your research focus as early as possible.** Use every course's readings/materials to actively ask: *how is this relevant to my research? How does this inform my literature review or research question?* The coursework moves quickly — without a well-defined focus, you won't be able to write a strong, achievable research question or know what literature to pursue.
5. **Program fit matters.** The Helms School's doctoral programs are explicitly, unabashedly Christian, with a mission to train "champions for Christ." Students should expect academic content to be considered in light of a biblical worldview, and should weigh program fit accordingly (regardless of personal faith background).
6. **The committee's role:** Your capstone chair/reader will tell you whether your project is viable, whether your research question is properly formulated, and whether your research design is appropriate — but they do not choose the topic or do the work for you.

## Relevance to This Capstone Project
Point #4 (narrow your focus early, let every course's material actively feed the literature review/research question) is exactly the practice this repository has been built to support since Week 1 — every coursework assignment has been deliberately steered toward the capstone topic, and `capstone/topic_overview.md`'s "Theoretical Framework" section exists specifically to keep that narrowing process visible and growing across future courses.
