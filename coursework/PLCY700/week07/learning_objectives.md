# Week 7 — Learning Objectives & Study Guide

## Overview (Instructor's Module Introduction, Verbatim)
In this module, you will consider causal theories — what causes a particular policy problem and what intervention will alleviate that problem — in the public policy context. These causal theories form the foundation upon which public policies are created. You will also practice the art of oral presentations required of all successful doctoral students.

## Learning Outcomes (Instructor's Official List)
Upon successful completion of this module, you will be able to:
1. Evaluate causal theories in public policy.
2. Assess efficiency versus equity in public policy.
3. Develop an oral presentation of your literature review.

**Core Learning Activity:** Record and post a 4–5 minute video oral presentation of your Week 4 Literature Assessment and Research Question, then post two peer-reply comments (150+ words each).

## Discussion: Oral Presentation Literature Review/Research Question — Official Instructions (Verbatim)

This final discussion asks you to post a video in which you give an oral presentation on your research proposal. You will then provide written feedback to your peers.

**Post-First:** This course utilizes the Post-First feature in all Discussions. This means you will only be able to read and interact with your classmates' threads after you have submitted your thread in response to the provided prompt.

### Assignment Instructions
Each doctoral student will complete one (1) video presentation at the conclusion of this course. The student will post one video of 4–5 minutes in length. The student must then post two (2) replies to colleague video posts of at least 150 words each.

Video presentations must cover every major component of the student's Literature Assessment and Research Question Assignment. Presentations must also address all feedback and correct any issues noted in the grading of the literature assessment assignment. Common themes, assumptions, and methodological approaches in the literature, areas of weakness or limitations, and the implications of the literature for your own policy interests should be clearly and properly delineated. Your unique, policy-related, scientific research question should be presented. Presentation of these components should be logical in flow.

Students should think of and structure this presentation as the introduction to an academic conference presentation. The main difference between a full conference presentation and this assigned presentation is that students will only have the introductory elements of the presentation (literature assessment/review and research question). As such, the focus of the presentation should be on the common themes in the assessed literature. See below for instructions on how to post a video to a Canvas Discussion.

After posting your video by 11:59 p.m. (ET) on Thursday of the assigned Module: Week, you must post two replies to your colleagues by 11:59 p.m. (ET) on Sunday of the assigned Module: Week. Reply threads are an opportunity for you to provide your colleagues with encouragement and constructive feedback on their research questions and literature evaluated. What does your colleague do well, and what might help develop their future research? Suggestions for improvement must be concrete, relevant, based in the concepts covered in the course, and phrased in a constructive and collegial manner.

## Content Requirements Checklist
The 4–5 minute video must clearly and logically cover, in order:
1. **Topic introduction** — the chosen public policy area of interest ("Leapfrog Advantage & AI Integration" in emerging-economy digital health).
2. **Common themes, assumptions, and methodological approaches** identified across the 10 assessed sources.
3. **Areas of weakness or limitations** in the literature assessed.
4. **Implications** of the literature for your own policy/dissertation interests.
5. **Your unique, policy-related, scientific research question**, stated clearly.
6. **Response to instructor feedback** — address and correct any issues noted in the grading of the Week 4 Literature Assessment (pending — no feedback received yet as of this writing; see [../week04/feedback/](../week04/feedback/)).

**Two peer replies (150+ words each):** concrete, constructive, collegial feedback on colleagues' research questions and literature — grounded in course concepts, not generic praise.

## Reading Guidance: Causal Theories & Efficiency vs. Equity
This module's course readings (causal theory of policy problems, efficiency vs. equity trade-offs) underpin *why* a research question matters — frame the presentation's research question in terms of the causal mechanism it investigates (e.g., does predictive AI + interoperable digital health infrastructure *cause* a reduction in reactive crisis spending?) and note where the dissertation's angle raises efficiency/equity tensions (e.g., resource allocation between infrastructure investment and immediate care access in emerging economies).
