# Week 5 — Learning Objectives & Study Guide

## Overview
Readings this week cover research ethics/proposal writing (Walliman, Ch. 6–7) and political decision-making/inducements (Stone, Ch. 10–11). Full chapter-by-chapter notes: [reading_notes.md](reading_notes.md).

## Learning Outcomes
- Explain why research ethics function as an epistemic (not merely administrative) foundation for valid scientific knowledge.
- Identify the forms of plagiarism, scientific fraud, and bias, and the safeguards (informed consent, IRB/REC review) that mitigate them.
- Describe the structural components of a doctoral research proposal and the iterative writing/note-management practices that support it.
- Contrast the rational-analytic model of decision-making (CBA, risk-benefit analysis, decision trees) with Stone's *polis* model of political decision-making.
- Explain how inducements (rewards/sanctions) function as symbolic and power-laden instruments, and the perverse incentives/goal displacement risks they create.
