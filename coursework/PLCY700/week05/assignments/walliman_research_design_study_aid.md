## 1. Research Integrity, Scientific Fraud, and Methodological Safeguards

### The Malcolm Pearce Case (Walliman, Box 6.1)

- **Offense:** Malcolm Pearce was convicted of **scientific fraud** (falsification and fabrication) for manufacturing clinical data, inventing a non-existent surgical operation, and fabricating patient trials in reproductive medicine.
- **Direct Risks of Misinformation:**
  - Dissemination of fabricated surgical techniques (such as relocating an ectopic pregnancy into the uterus) posed severe risks of internal hemorrhage and maternal mortality if attempted by other clinicians.
  - Prescribing unproven or harmful clinical regimens based on fabricated polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) trials.
  - Imparting false hope to vulnerable patients facing critical medical decisions.
  - Misallocating scientific and institutional resources toward futile replication efforts.
- **Built-in Scientific Safeguards:**
  - **Replication (Repeatability):** Independent researchers reproducing procedures to test whether reported results are genuine and reliable.
  - **Peer Review:** Critical pre-publication appraisal of theoretical plausibility, data validity, and methodology by independent domain specialists.
  - **Methodological Transparency & Audit Trails:** Retention and public availability of raw laboratory logs, patient records, and empirical code.
  - **Institutional Review Boards (IRB) / Ethics Committees:** Mandatory independent oversight and ethical registration before human subject intervention.
- **Feasibility of an External "Fraud Squad":**
  - **Core Limitations:** Unrealistic as a primary defense due to the sheer volume of global research, extreme hyper-specialization across fields, difficulty in proving deliberate intent versus honest methodological error, and cross-border jurisdictional constraints. Scientific integrity relies fundamentally on internal institutional governance, transparent data practices, and peer replication.

## 2. Survey Instrument & Questionnaire Design: Critical Flaws

Survey questions must adhere to strict principles of clarity, mutual exclusivity, collective exhaustiveness, and accessible readability.

| Flawed Question Example | Identified Methodological Flaw | Principle of Rigorous Design |
|---|---|---|
| *"Where do you come from?"* | **Construct Ambiguity / Vague Interpretation:** Multiple competing meanings (country of birth, ethnicity, current residence, hometown). | Instruments must specify exact variables (e.g., *"What is your country of birth?"*). |
| *"How many children do you have? 0, 1, 2, 3, 4"* | **Not Collectively Exhaustive:** Omission of categories for respondents with five or more children; undefined scope of "children." | Categories must include every possible respondent state (e.g., open numerical box or a "5+" category). |
| *"Do you like modern architecture? Score 0–100"* | **Overly Granular Scale & Subjective Term:** Excessive cognitive burden distinguishing points on a 101-point scale; undefined terminology. | Use validated 5- or 7-point Likert scales with clear anchors and operationalized definitions. |
| *"When did you last drink a bottle of champagne: yesterday, last week, last month, last year?"* | **Loaded / Presumptuous & Discontinuous Intervals:** Falsely assumes behavior; lacks "Never" or "N/A" options; leaves temporal gaps. | Avoid loaded assumptions; provide comprehensive categorical coverage including negative and non-applicable options. |
| Excerpt with dense post-structuralist jargon asking to classify abstract theory. | **Cognitive Overload & Inaccessible Jargon:** Acts as an elitist comprehension test rather than measuring participant views. | Questions must match the language and reading level of the target sample. |

## 3. Experimental Modeling: Inputs vs. Outputs

Every empirical experiment or computer simulation must distinguish between **independent/control parameters (data needed)** and **dependent/outcome variables (data produced)**.

```
Inputs (Data Needed) ────────► [Experimental System / Model] ────────► Outputs (Data Produced)
(Independent & Controlled)                                              (Dependent & Measured)
```

### Empirical Scenarios

1. **Hard-Boiled Egg Experiment:**
   - *Data Needed (Inputs):* Egg size/mass (grams), post-lay age, baseline egg temperature, water volume, and constant boiling temperature.
   - *Data Produced (Outputs):* Cooking time required to reach the hard-boiled threshold, verified by core yolk temperature and texture firmness.
2. **Bus Route Simulation Model (Bus Bunching):**
   - *Data Needed (Inputs):* Route distance, stop locations, dispatch headways, free-flow speeds, traffic congestion distributions, and passenger boarding/alighting rates.
   - *Data Produced (Outputs):* Variance in headway gaps between successive buses, frequency and geographic clusters of three-bus bunching, trip durations, and queue delays.
3. **Glass Window Impact Experiment:**
   - *Data Needed (Inputs):* Framing materials (aluminum, wood, steel), glazing/fastening methods, rebate depth, glass thickness/type, and impactor mass/velocity.
   - *Data Produced (Outputs):* Kinetic energy failure thresholds (Joules), deflection strain distributions, and categorical failure modes (shattering vs. dislodgement).

## 4. Qualitative Data Collection Methods: Interview Typology

Qualitative methods vary along a continuum of researcher control, standardization, and emergent participant narrative:

- **Structured Interviews:** Standardized, scripted, closed/fixed questions delivered in identical order; designed for high reliability, comparability, and quantitative coding.
- **Semi-Structured Interviews:** Guided by a thematic framework of open-ended questions; allows the researcher the flexibility to alter sequence and use spontaneous follow-up probes.
- **Open (Unstructured) Interviews:** Conversational and emergent; guided by broad topical prompts where the participant largely directs the dialogue and priorities.
- **Accounts (Narratives / Life Histories):** Continuous, self-directed storytelling where participants explain, contextualize, and morally justify their experiences in their own terminology.

## 5. Sampling Strategy & Population Representation

- **Optimal Sampling Technique:** **Random (probability) sampling** provides the most reliable representation of the entire population because every sampling unit possesses a known, non-zero probability of selection, minimizing systematic bias.
- **Considerations When Selecting Random Sampling Methods:**
  - Absolute size and structural characteristics of the target population.
  - Availability and accuracy of an exhaustive sampling frame.
  - Geographic dispersion and logistical accessibility.
  - Degree of internal subgroup heterogeneity (determining whether simple random, stratified, or cluster sampling is warranted).
  - Available budget, labor, and timeline constraints.
