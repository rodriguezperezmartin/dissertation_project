# Week 1 — Learning Objectives & Study Guide

## Overview
Module 1 introduces the foundations of public policy, including causal theories/causal stories (Stone) and the policy process (Birkland), through a discussion-based introduction assignment.

**Core Learning Activity:** Post a discussion thread (1,000–1,250 words) introducing your policy interests/Christian worldview (Part 1, ≤200 words) and evaluating Angstadt's (2020) use of Stone's "causal stories" framework (Part 2), then post two peer replies (400–500 words each, 2+ scholarly citations each).

## Discussion: Introduction to Public Policy — Official Instructions (Verbatim)

The student will post one thread of 1,000–1,250 words by 11:59 p.m. (ET) on Thursday of Module 1: Week 1. The student must then post two replies of 400–500 words by 11:59 p.m. (ET) on Sunday of Module 1: Week 1. For each thread, students must support their assertions with relevant scholarly citations in APA format. Each reply must incorporate at least two scholarly citations in APA format. Acceptable sources include the course readings, Angstadt and Stone articles, and scholarly sources, such as books and peer-reviewed journal articles.

### Prompt
**Part 1:** Briefly, in 1 short paragraph of 200 words or less, elaborate on your specific policy interests and the ways in which your Christian worldview or calling have shaped those interests.

**Part 2:** Evaluate the Angstadt article's use of Stone's "causal stories." How successful was Angstadt in utilizing this methodology for policy agendas? Support your assertions with evidence from both articles.

**Post-First:** This course utilizes the Post-First feature in all Discussions. This means you will only be able to read and interact with your classmates' threads after you have submitted your thread in response to the provided prompt.

Submit your thread by 11:59 p.m. (ET) on Thursday of Module 1: Week 1.

## Submitted Thread — Topic & Sources
**Policy interest:** Fiscal/monetary policy to stimulate household income growth in emerging Latin American economies (Honduras, El Salvador, Mexico), using post-WWII U.S. Keynesian/classical economic policy as a comparative control case.

**Part 2 analysis:** Evaluates Angstadt's (2020) application of Stone's (1989) "causal stories" framework to the American Prairie Reserve (APR) vs. Ranchers Stewardship Alliance (RSA) land-management conflict in Montana — argues Angstadt's key contribution is showing that *strategically omitting the moral/blame dimension* from a causal story can be more effective than Stone's dichotomous framework in complex, multi-stakeholder, cooperation-dependent scenarios.

**Sources cited:**
- Angstadt, J. M. (2020). Applying Stone in a western landscape: Ranchers, conservationists, and causal stories in the "American Serengeti". *The Review of Policy Research, 37*(2), 6–8.
- Birkland, T. A. (2016). *An introduction to the policy process: Theories, concepts, and models of public policy making* (4th ed.). Routledge.
- Stone, D. A. (1989). Causal stories and the formation of policy agendas. *Political Science Quarterly, 104*(2), 281–300.

**Word count note:** Part 1 is ~196 words (within the ≤200 limit); the full thread (Part 1 + Part 2) is ~1,398 words, running above the 1,000–1,250 word target for the thread — worth keeping in mind if asked to trim in a future revision or similar assignment.
