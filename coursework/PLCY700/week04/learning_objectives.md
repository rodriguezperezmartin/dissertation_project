# Week 4 — Learning Objectives & Study Guide

## Overview
Assessing current academic literature is the first step in creating your own unique contribution to the literature. This week you will learn how to write a basic literature review and how to develop a sound, scientific research question from that literature. You will also consider various influences on policy agendas.

## Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion of this module, you will be able to:
- Evaluate a body of academic literature in your chosen public policy area of interest.
- Develop a scientific research question from your chosen body of academic literature.
- Identify sources of political power and their influence on policy agendas.
- Compare and contrast distributive, regulatory, and redistributive policies.

## Assignment Instructions — Literature Assessment and Research Question

### Overview
Before a scholar can contribute to the academic literature, it is first necessary to assess the existing literature. This assessment ultimately takes the form of a thorough Literature Review, which for the dissertation may include several dozen sources — one full dissertation chapter will be devoted to it. At this point in the program, the task is a much briefer **Literature Assessment**: less comprehensive and detailed than a full Literature Review, but it helps develop the habit of thinking broadly about a body of scholarly literature and drawing a sound Research Question from it. Chosen thoughtfully, this assignment is a first step toward identifying literature relevant to the dissertation.

### Instructions
Assess **ten or more scholarly sources** (peer-reviewed academic journal articles) related to **one policy topic** of interest — ideally a topic that may be researched for the dissertation. The assessment should:
- Explore common themes, assumptions, or approaches in the sources covered.
- Assess common weaknesses or limitations in the sources covered.
- Identify the implications of the Literature Assessment for future research:
  - Are there common sources cited across this literature that seem highly influential and worth attention?
  - Are there common conclusions in this literature about future research that might be warranted?
  - Are there common errors or oversights that may need to be corrected?
- End with a **policy-related, appropriate, scientific Research Question** drawn from the literature reviewed.

**Format:** Current APA format. **Length:** 2,000–2,500 words (not including cover page, bibliography, or footnotes). **Note:** checked for originality via Turnitin.

### Grading Rubric (100 pts total)
| Criterion | Points | Advanced (top band) |
|---|---|---|
| Content: Identification of common elements in literature assessed | 40 | 3+ common elements/themes, clearly evaluated for significance; all sources scholarly/academic |
| Content: Identification of gaps or weaknesses in literature assessed | 30 | 3+ gaps/weaknesses, clearly evaluated for significance |
| Content: Evaluation of implications for further research | 30 | 3+ significant implications (key texts, theories, methodological approaches, research trends) |
| Content: Scientific Research Question | 23 | Written as a question, testable and falsifiable, follows naturally from the literature |
| Structure: APA formatting | 17 | Accurate and consistent APA formatting |
| Structure: Writing Mechanics | 18 | Clear, concise, correct grammar/punctuation/spelling; 2,000–2,500 words |
| Structure: Organization | 17 | Logical arrangement, clear points, easy to follow |
| **Total** | **175** | |

> Note: the rubric's stated per-criterion max points (40+30+30+23+17+18+17 = 175) — recorded as posted by Dr. Boykin.

Use APA style consistently; reference guide: [Liberty University APA Writing Guide](https://libguides.liberty.edu/c.php?g=1036190&p=7529654)

### Writing Tip (from Dr. Boykin's announcement email)
Avoid organizing the assessment paper-by-paper (one source after another). Instead, **organize topically** — group sources by theme so the reader understands what the research says about a particular area, where sources agree/disagree, and how they differ in methodology and conclusions.

## Chosen Topic — The "Leapfrog" Advantage & AI Integration

This is a **strong candidate for the refined dissertation thesis** — testing a specific, near-term financial outcome (reduced reactive crisis spending) rather than waiting on lagging macroeconomic indicators, which sidesteps the "missing historical data" problem in emerging economies.

**Final Research Question:**
> To what extent does the implementation of predictive AI models and interoperable digital health architectures reduce reactive crisis spending in public health interventions within emerging economies?

**Core angle:** Emerging economies have a structural advantage — no legacy IT systems to work around — allowing them to jump straight into interoperable, AI-assisted health architectures. This tests whether applying modern technical stacks (agentic AI architectures, RAG frameworks, cloud-native ML pipelines, predictive models trained on first-world data but fine-tuned for third-world contexts) shifts government/NGO spending from reactive crisis management to proactive, targeted aid. Can be tested via pilot programs, synthetic data, or smaller localized NGO datasets rather than a decade-long macroeconomic study — **the technology itself becomes the policy intervention.**

**Strategic alignment:** Bridges technical data engineering (MLOps/AI) with international economic policy; appeals directly to foreign aid allocation strategy — ties into the "Government Service and Diplomacy" and "Modernizing Healthcare Infrastructure" goals in [dissertation/topic_overview.md](../../../dissertation/topic_overview.md).

### Search Strategy (Jerry Falwell Library — target 10 peer-reviewed sources)
- **Search keywords:** "predictive AI public health," "interoperable health systems," "technology leapfrogging emerging markets," "healthcare resource allocation," "crisis spending"
- **Area 1 — The "Leapfrog" Concept:** papers on developing nations bypassing legacy IT systems (analogous to landlines → mobile phones, applied to healthcare data)
- **Area 2 — Resource Allocation:** economic papers on how AI/data-driven platforms change government/NGO allocation of targeted aid vs. emergency relief
- **Area 3 — Cross-Context AI Application:** technical policy papers evaluating models trained on developed-world data deployed in low-resource environments
