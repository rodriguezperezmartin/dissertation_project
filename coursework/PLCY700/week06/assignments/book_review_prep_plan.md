**Purpose of this document:** A working plan to (1) understand the assignment/grading requirements in plain language, (2) give you a focused scanning checklist so your refresh pass through *Bureaucracy* targets exactly what the rubric rewards, and (3) provide a rough outline skeleton to fill in yourself as you scan and think. ## 1. The Assignment, In Plain English

You are writing a **1,500–2,000 word critical book review** with 5 required moves, in this order:

1. **Hook the reader** — an opening that makes someone want to keep reading about this book.
2. **State the thesis** — what is Wilson's one big argument?
3. **Cover the main themes** — what are the recurring ideas that support that argument?
4. **Explain the method** — is Wilson's evidence qualitative or quantitative, and how does he build his case?
5. **Judge the conclusions** — do they hold up? What's missing or weak?
6. **Place the book in the field** — how does it connect to what you've read in this course (Birkland, Stone, Week 4 sources)?

Format: APA 7, no long block quotes (summarize in your own words), checked by Turnitin.

## 2. Grading Rubric, Simplified (175 pts total)

| # | What's graded | Pts | In plain terms |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Central Thesis & Book Choice | 30 | Did you clearly say what the book argues, and is it the approved book? |
| 2 | Main Themes & Methods | **40 (biggest chunk)** | Did you explain the themes AND clearly say qualitative vs. quantitative AND explain *how* Wilson builds his case? |
| 3 | Evaluation of Conclusions | 30 | Are the conclusions well-supported? What weaknesses did you catch, backed by the book itself? |
| 4 | Place in the Field | 23 | Did you tie the book to specific course readings/citations? |
| 5 | APA Formatting | 17 | Clean, consistent APA. |
| 6 | Writing Mechanics | 18 | Clear prose, correct grammar, 1,500–2,000 words. |
| 7 | Organization | 17 | Logical flow, easy to follow. |

**Takeaway:** Criterion #2 (themes + methodology) is worth the most — spend your scanning time making sure you can clearly state (a) 3–4 main themes and (b) exactly how Wilson supports them (qualitative, comparative case studies — not statistics).

## 3. Scanning Checklist — What To Look For As You Skim

Since you already have full chapter notes, use this scan pass to **re-locate and confirm** the handful of things the rubric actually grades, rather than re-reading everything closely. Check these specific spots in the book/summary:

- [ ] **Preface / Introduction** — Wilson's own statement of purpose and thesis (this is often the clearest single-sentence version of the thesis you'll cite).
- [ ] **Table of Contents** — confirms the book's structural logic (Part I: Organizations, Part II: Operators/Managers/Executives, Part III: Context, Part IV: Change). Use this to pick your 3–4 themes so they map onto the book's own structure.
- [ ] **First and last paragraph of each Part's opening chapter** — where Wilson usually states the argument for that section.
- [ ] **Chapter 1 (Armies, Prisons, and Schools)** — foundational thesis statement; almost certainly your central-thesis citation.
- [ ] **Chapter 9 (Compliance)** — the Production/Procedural/Craft/Coping organization typology; a strong methodology/theme anchor and your direct bridge to Stone's *Policy Paradox* Ch. 9 (Numbers).
- [ ] **Chapter 10 (Turf)** and **Chapter 13 (Congress)** — institutional friction/coordination themes; strong dissertation tie-in material.
- [ ] **Final chapter (Bureaucracy and the Public Interest / conclusion)** — Wilson's own summary of conclusions and any limitations he acknowledges himself.
- [ ] **Anywhere Wilson explicitly says "I" or "my argument"** — flags his own methodological self-description (useful for the qualitative/quantitative discussion).
- [ ] **Buck (1992) critique** ([../original_src_files/Buck-BureaucracyGovernmentAgencies-1992.pdf](../original_src_files/Buck-BureaucracyGovernmentAgencies-1992.pdf)) — an outside expert's take on weaknesses; scan this for 1–2 citable critiques you can cross-check against the book itself.
