## Key Takeaways / Corrections to Internalize
1. **Ends vs. means:** Broad agreement on the Founders' shared *end* (the rights of man, stated in the Declaration) permits varied, even conflicting, *means/prudential judgments* about how to achieve it — compromise on means is not compromise on principle. Ties to Jefferson's First Inaugural line, "we are all federalists, we are all republicans."
2. **Natural Right vs. natural rights:** The Founders acted in accordance with classical **Natural Right** (what is just always and everywhere, a matter of prudence in application) rather than a modern, individualistic doctrine of **natural rights** alone. Their prudence can be criticized, but not their grounding in justice.
3. **States/nations do not hold rights — only people do.** Distinguish **natural right** from **civil rights** when discussing nations/entities "in formation."
4. **Legitimacy = consent of the governed**, per the Declaration of Independence — this is the Founders' own definitional anchor, distinct from academic/contemporary legitimacy theories (e.g., Sartori's garantiste constitutions, Loughlin's codified-rights argument) discussed this week.
5. **No "living Constitution":** In the American context, a "living Constitution" is a contradiction — it amounts to having no constitution at all (binding law that can be reinterpreted at will is not binding law). Reference: Bradley Watson, *Living Constitution, Dying Faith*.
