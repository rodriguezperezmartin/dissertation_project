## Course Description (Official)
The course explores the key actors, events and content of the American Founding period and provides an application of Biblical principles of statesmanship as discussed in PLCY 701. Special emphasis is placed on the religious, social economic and philosophical influences on U.S. constitutional design as well as significant crises and inconsistencies between ideals and practice. Study will span the formative period from the granting of colonial charters to the ratification of the U.S. Constitution and Bill of Rights and the early years of the republic. A combination of historical textbook(s), scholarly journal articles and primary documents will be examined.

## Rationale (Official)
Americans often view the United States Constitution as a singular document that created a *Novus Ordo Seclorum* — a "new order of the ages." Yet this Constitution represented a culmination of hundreds of years of theoretical and political developments from the English constitutional tradition. And since its adoption, the United States Constitution has prompted the development of constitutionalism as a global norm, with virtually every nation in the world having adopted some form of constitutional government.

This paradox raises questions relevant for the study and development of American public policy. Such questions are especially pressing as the post-Cold War consensus about America's role in the world has dissolved. Most notably, does (or should) the United States Constitution be seen as representing a popular consensus regarding the development of the political institutions and practices of a particular people with a particular interest in constitutional self-government? Or does (or should) it be seen as one of many constitutions that have been implemented around the globe which continue to converge upon an elite consensus about liberal and democratic political norms?

This course encourages the student to explore these questions through examination of key primary sources from the history of the development of precedents to the Constitution, the Constitution itself, and debate about its ratification and interpretation. The student will be asked to reflect on the implications of his or her inquiry for public policies in the area of his or her research interest.

## Course Learning Outcomes (Official)
Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:
- **A.** Discuss the Anglo-American tradition of constitutionalism, identifying key features and developments as they appeared in colonial, state, and national founding documents, constitutions, and political writings.
- **B.** Compare and contrast the United States Constitution against modern conceptions of constitutionalism.
- **C.** Define key concepts like liberty and equality as various groups of Americans understood them during the colonial and founding eras.
- **D.** Assess the relevance of various definitions of liberty to conceptions of republicanism and rights as they were understood during the colonial and founding eras.
- **E.** Summarize how key constitutional provisions, institutions, or norms continue to frame debate or public policy developments in your area of public policy interest.

## Course Assignments (Official)
- **Course Requirements Checklist** — complete after reading the syllabus/Student Expectations; required in Week 1 or risk being dropped from the course for non-attendance.
- **Discussions (6 total, 100 pts each = 600 pts):** one thread (≥750 words, course-related knowledge) + 3 replies to classmates (≥250 words each) per discussion.
- **Annotated Bibliography Assignment (100 pts):** one paragraph (5–8 sentences) defining the student's research interest, culminating in a provisional research question, plus identification/assessment of 8 peer-reviewed sources addressing constitutional questions related to that research interest. **Strong opportunity to steer directly toward the capstone topic** (healthcare digital infrastructure & data-driven public policy in emerging economies) — find the constitutional/founding-era angle that connects.
- **Constitutionalism and American Public Policy Assignment (300 pts):** 8-page research-based paper, current APA format, on an issue of American constitutionalism covered in the course as it relates to the student's public policy interest; at least 12 peer-reviewed academic references.
