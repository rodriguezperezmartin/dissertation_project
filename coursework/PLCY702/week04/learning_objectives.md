## Module 4 Introduction

Federalism has been an irreducible feature of American constitutionalism since its colonial era. In this module you will explore the origins and significance of the federal union as it existed under British rule and as it developed into a confederation of "free and independent states."

---

## Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of this module, you will be able to:

1. **Identify precedents and examples of confederation in the colonial experience** — recognizing the federal principle (division of power between central and local authority) as rooted in colonial political practice, English common law traditions, and biblical/classical precedent.

2. **Explain the origins and key features of the Articles of Confederation** — understanding why the Articles were adopted after independence, what problems they were designed to solve, what structural features they embodied, and what practical difficulties emerged in their application.

3. **Assess the significance of the wave of constitutional activity that followed independence** — evaluating how the experiences under the Articles (and the states' constitutional experiments) shaped the design of the U.S. Constitution and informed the Framers' understanding of federalism, separation of powers, and checks and balances.

**Three primary sources anchor this week:**

1. **Peace Pact (Articles of Confederation)** — Sets the stage for why the initial post-independence government was impractical. The Declaration's invocation laid the groundwork for independence; it would be invoked again in creating the Constitution we have today (the second such use of the Declaration's ideas). Federalism looms large — partly national, partly federal — and understanding the Articles is essential to understanding why the Constitution became necessary.

2. **De Tocqueville, Chapter 5** — Explores the sovereignty of the people: how is it expressed? De T examines the people from small to large scale. States (localities) were still strong in 1835. Are they today? This is fragile. De T notes how rare it is for local communities to exercise real sovereignty in modern times. His observations on administrative centralization are crucial: how do governmental centralization and administrative centralization combine and mutually reinforce each other?

3. **McClellan chapters** — Addresses colonial confederations, the Declaration, and the Articles. (Prof. Root includes critical commentary on McClellan's interpretive choices — read critically, check primary sources.)

**A cautionary tale from Adams:** He and Jefferson were among the grandest thinkers of their time. The question for our post-constitutional era — a grave time indeed — is whether a people can ever become one people, or whether we are inevitably all tribal.

### Assignment Overview

The Annotated Bibliography serves dual purposes:
1. **Narrowing your research question** — connecting your public policy interest to founding-era constitutional principles
2. **Building your capstone literature foundation** — identifying at least 10 peer-reviewed sources on constitutional issues relevant to your research

### Required Components

1. **Research Interest Statement (1 paragraph, complete but concise — aim for 150–200 words)**
   - Define your public policy research interest in the context of American constitutionalism and your doctoral program
   - Include your doctoral program and cognate area
   - Explain how your research interest is consistent with your chosen doctoral program and cognate
   - Culminate in a clear, provisional research question connecting founding-era constitutional principles to your policy area
   - **Capstone opportunity:** Frame the question around healthcare digital infrastructure + emerging economies + constitutional constraints
     - Example 1: "How do federalism principles and constitutional limits on administrative power shape digital health infrastructure adoption in emerging democracies?"
     - Example 2: "To what extent do traditional constitutional protections of local autonomy explain different healthcare policy choices in Guatemala vs. Mexico?"
     - Example 3: "What role do constitutional protections of property rights and due process play in shaping resistance to centralized health data mandates in emerging economies?"

2. **Annotated Bibliography (At Least 10 Peer-Reviewed Sources)**
   - Each source: APA 7 citation + annotation (200–250 words)
   - Annotation should include:
     - **Central Thesis/Research Question:** What is the source's main argument or research question?
     - **Methodology/Approach:** How does the author support their thesis? What analytical framework or evidence is used?
     - **Key Findings:** What are the central conclusions?
     - **Relevance/Applicability:** How does this source address constitutional principles relevant to your research question? How does this inform your capstone/policy research on healthcare digital infrastructure in emerging economies?
   - **Template Reference:** See [REFERENCE_Sample-Annotated-Bibliography-graduate.pdf](REFERENCE_Sample-Annotated-Bibliography-graduate.pdf) for Liberty University's official format example (title page, alphabetization, annotation structure, APA 7 formatting)

### Recommended Sources Strategy

**Find sources addressing:**
- Federalism and state/national power division (esp. health policy context)
- Constitutional limits on administrative authority
- Individual liberty vs. collective/state authority (from Week 3)
- Due process, property rights, or equal protection as constraints on policy implementation
- Comparative constitutional frameworks (US vs. emerging democracies' approaches)
- Republican self-governance and civic participation

**Search databases:**
- JSTOR (Liberty Library)
- Google Scholar
- Project MUSE (Liberty Library)
- American Political Science Association (APSA) journal archive
- Constitutional law journals and policy journals

## Capstone Connection

**Week 4's federalism focus directly illuminates your capstone research question:**

- **Federalism principle:** Division of power between national and local authorities — does this protect or hinder digital health infrastructure implementation in emerging democracies?
- **Constitutional limits:** How do property rights, due process, or administrative constraints on government power affect health data governance in different countries?
- **Republican self-governance:** Do communities retain meaningful local control over health policy adoption, or does centralized mandate override local sovereignty?

**Case study parallels:**
- **Guatemala:** Decentralized digital health approach — aligns with federalism principle of local autonomy; emphasizes stakeholder participation
- **Mexico 2026 mandate:** Centralized national mandate — raises questions about state/national power, administrative centralization, and community consent

Use this week's reading and your Annotated Bibliography research to deepen your understanding of these tensions and strengthen your capstone's constitutional/theoretical foundation.
