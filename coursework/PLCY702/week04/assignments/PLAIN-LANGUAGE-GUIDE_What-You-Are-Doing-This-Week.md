## What is an "Annotated Bibliography"? (In Plain English)

An **Annotated Bibliography** is simply a **list of sources + summaries of what each source says**.

Think of it like this:
- **Bibliography** = a list of books/articles you've read (like citations you'd see at the end of a research paper)
- **Annotated** = you add a **summary paragraph** after each citation explaining what that source is about and why it matters for YOUR research

---

## What Does One Entry Look Like?

Here's a real example (simplified):

```
Hendrickson, D. C. (2003). Peace pact: The lost world of the American 
founding. University Press of Kansas.

Hendrickson argues that the American founding was fundamentally a 
"peace treaty" among states that could have gone to war with each 
other — not primarily a document creating one unified nation. He 
demonstrates this through international relations analysis, showing 
how the Articles of Confederation and later the Constitution were 
designed to prevent interstate rivalries like those in Europe. 
Hendrickson's thesis is relevant to my research because it suggests 
that federal structures exist partly to prevent conflict between 
different jurisdictions. In healthcare, this implies that if digital 
health systems are imposed centrally (top-down), sub-national 
authorities might resist or compete, while federal approaches 
respecting local autonomy might reduce administrative conflict and 
improve adoption.
```

**That's one entry.** The first part is the citation (the source). The second part is your summary (the annotation).
