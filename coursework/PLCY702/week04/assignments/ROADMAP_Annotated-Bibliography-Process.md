## Phase 2 — Draft the Research Interest Statement (28 pts of the rubric)

This is the highest-weighted single criterion. Do this *before* searching for sources, because your research question should drive your search — not the other way around.

**Step 1: Freewrite (10–15 minutes, no editing).**
Answer these questions in rough prose:
- What specific public policy problem in healthcare digital infrastructure (in emerging economies) am I most drawn to?
- Which constitutional principle from this week (federalism, limits on administrative power, local autonomy, interstate/international coordination) connects most naturally to that problem?
- Why does this connection matter for public policy — not just as an academic curiosity, but as something with real implementation consequences?

**Step 2: Select one of the candidate framings (or synthesize your own).**
From [ASSIGNMENT_DETAILS_Annotated-Bibliography.md](ASSIGNMENT_DETAILS_Annotated-Bibliography.md):
- Federalism/administrative-power framing: *"How do federalism principles and constitutional limits on administrative power shape digital health infrastructure adoption in emerging democracies?"*
- Comparative autonomy framing: *"To what extent do traditional constitutional protections of local autonomy explain different healthcare policy choices in Guatemala vs. Mexico?"*
- Property rights/due process framing: *"What role do constitutional protections of property rights and due process play in shaping resistance to centralized health data mandates in emerging economies?"*

**Step 3: Write the full paragraph (150–200 words).**
Required elements, in this order:
1. Opening sentence(s): state the general public policy issue (healthcare digital infrastructure adoption/implementation in emerging economies).
2. Middle: narrow to the constitutional/federalism angle driving your interest this week (cite at least one Week 4 reading by name/author to ground it).
3. State your doctoral program (Ph.D. in Public Policy) and cognate explicitly.
4. Explain briefly *why* this research interest fits your program/cognate.
5. Close with a clearly stated, single-sentence provisional research question.

**Step 4: Self-check against rubric language.**
- Is the research interest "clearly defined and viable"? (Not vague/general.)
- Is it "clearly related to public policy" and to your "doctoral program and cognate"?
- Is it a coherent paragraph (not a few disconnected sentences)?

## Phase 3 — Literature Search: Identify 10+ Peer-Reviewed Sources (15 pts of the rubric)

**Step 1: Translate your research question into search terms.**
Starting points (expand/refine based on your Phase 2 research question):
- "federalism" + "health policy"
- "constitutional limits" + "administrative power" / "administrative state"
- "centralization" OR "decentralization" + "healthcare" OR "digital health"
- "local autonomy" + "policy implementation"
- "emerging economies" OR "developing countries" + "digital health infrastructure"
- "property rights" OR "due process" + "health data" OR "health policy"
- "republican self-governance" + "health governance"
- Country-specific: "Guatemala health system decentralization", "Mexico health policy centralization"

**Step 2: Search in priority order.**
1. **JSTOR** (via Liberty Library) — best for constitutional law and political theory sources
2. **Google Scholar** — broadest coverage; use "cited by" feature to chain into related work
3. **Project MUSE** (via Liberty Library) — strong for interdisciplinary policy/humanities crossover
4. **APSA journal archive** — political science specific
5. **Health policy journals directly:** *Health Affairs*, *Health Policy and Planning*, *Journal of Health Economics*, *Policy Studies Journal*
6. **Constitutional law journals directly:** *Constitutional Commentary*, *Journal of Constitutional Law*

**Step 3: Screen each candidate source before committing to it.**
For every source you consider, verify:
- [ ] Peer-reviewed (check the journal's review policy if unsure — avoid trade publications, think-tank blogs, or non-refereed working papers unless clearly labeled as such)
- [ ] Clearly relevant to your stated research question (not just tangentially related)
- [ ] Accessible in full text (confirm you can actually read/cite it, not just an abstract)

**Step 4: Build a running source list (10–14 candidates) before writing annotations.**
Over-collect slightly (aim for 12–14 candidates) so you can drop the 2–4 weakest once you see how your 10 best sources cohere together thematically.

**Recommended practice:** Use RefWorks (free via Liberty) to store citations and draft annotations directly in the "Abstract" field — this keeps your citation manager and bibliography draft in sync from the start.

## Phase 4 — Write the Annotations (27 pts of the rubric)

For each of your 10+ final sources, write a 200–250 word annotation with four required components, in this order:

1. **Central thesis/research question** of the source (1–2 sentences) — what is this source actually arguing or asking?
2. **Methodology/approach** (2–3 sentences) — how does the author support their thesis? (empirical study, comparative case analysis, legal/doctrinal analysis, historical narrative, etc.)
3. **Key findings** (1–2 sentences) — what did the author conclude?
4. **Relevance/applicability to your research question** (2–3 sentences) — explicitly connect this source back to YOUR Phase 2 research question and your capstone's healthcare digital infrastructure focus. This is the component most often shortchanged — do not skip or rush it.

**Quality check for each annotation:**
- [ ] Does it avoid vague, generic language ("this source discusses policy") in favor of specific claims?
- [ ] Is the word count in the 200–250 range (not noticeably under or over)?
- [ ] Have you paraphrased in your own words rather than copying language from the abstract (Turnitin will check)?
- [ ] Does the final sentence or two explicitly name your research question or capstone topic?

## Phase 5 — Assemble, Format, and Proofread (30 pts of the rubric)

**Document structure:**
1. **Title page** — your name, course name (PLCY 702: Liberty and American Constitutionalism), instructor name (Prof. Root), date
2. **Research Interest** section/heading with your Phase 2 paragraph
3. **Annotated Bibliography** — new page, sources alphabetized by author's last name, each citation followed immediately by its annotation

**Formatting checklist:**
- [ ] All citations in correct APA 7 format (hanging indent, correct capitalization, correct punctuation)
- [ ] Alphabetical order by first author's last name
- [ ] Consistent font/spacing throughout (match APA 7 defaults: 12pt Times New Roman or similar, double-spaced, 1" margins)
- [ ] No orphaned annotations (every citation has its annotation directly beneath it)

**Proofreading pass (do this last, ideally after a break from the document):**
- [ ] Read the research interest paragraph aloud — does it flow coherently?
- [ ] Spot-check 2–3 annotations for grammar/spelling errors
- [ ] Verify every in-text claim about a source is accurate to what that source actually says (don't overstate findings)
- [ ] Run a grammar checker (e.g., Grammarly) or ask a peer/writing center reviewer for a final pass
- [ ] Confirm word counts are in range for both the research interest paragraph (150–200 words) and each annotation (200–250 words)
