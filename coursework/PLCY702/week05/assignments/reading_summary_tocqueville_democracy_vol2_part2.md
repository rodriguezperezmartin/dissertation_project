## I. Executive Summary

Tocqueville argues that democratic peoples love both liberty and equality, but their passion for equality is more ardent, more constant, and harder to satisfy. Political liberty has existed in many ages, including aristocratic ones. Equality of conditions is the distinctive fact of a democratic age, and it is the passion that drives the people. When the two conflict, democratic peoples will give up liberty to keep equality, and they will accept servitude if all are equal under one master.

---

## II. Why Equality Wins the Passion of the People (pp. 872–880)

**Thesis.** Equality of conditions shapes democratic life more than liberty does, and it wins the people's enduring passion.

**Key mechanisms.**
- *Distinctive fact of the age:* liberty has appeared in many periods, but equality of conditions is what defines a democratic society.
- *Asymmetry of costs:* the disorders of too much liberty are immediate and visible, and its benefits arrive slowly. The pleasures of equality are everyday and effortless, and its dangers, such as centralization, arrive quietly.
- *Accessibility versus vigilance:* liberty demands constant civic effort. Equality offers small daily gratifications that bind everyone to it without exertion.
- *Equality in servitude:* Tocqueville warns that democratic peoples want equality in liberty, and if they cannot have it, they still want equality in slavery. They can tolerate poverty or centralized despotism, but not aristocratic privilege.

**Constitutional takeaway.** This chapter sets up Tocqueville's later warning about administrative centralization and "soft despotism." Citizens who prize equality of condition may hand more power to a uniform central state to enforce it. Federalism, active local institutions, and constitutional checks are needed to keep equality from swallowing liberty.

---

## III. Connection to Fischer

Fischer's four folkways show that colonial America did not begin with one concept of equality or liberty. Tocqueville explains what happens when those older orders give way to a single democratic social state. In that state, the passion for equality grows, and only decentralized, local self-government keeps that passion compatible with liberty.
