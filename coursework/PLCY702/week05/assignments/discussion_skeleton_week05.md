### Section 1: Egalitarian features noted by European observers (about 200 words)
- Crèvecoeur, "What Is an American?": no "great lords" ruling a herd of people; the "new man" who sheds old prejudices. (Cite the reading.)
- Tocqueville, Vol. II, Pt. II, Ch. 1: "equality of conditions" as a social fact, not equality of outcome. (Cite Mansfield & Winthrop, 2000, once the page is confirmed.)

### Section 2: Jefferson's political equality (about 250 words)
- *Summary View* (1774): rights "given equally and independently to all"; Parliament's claim to govern 4 million "every individual of whom is equal."
- Declaration (1776): "all men are created equal"; equality as the ground for a separate and equal station among nations.
- Prompt: Where does Jefferson's equality overlap with the observers' social equality (no hereditary rank)? Where does it differ (a claim about legitimate government and the rights of peoples, not a description of conditions)?
- Source placeholders (confirmed): **Boulton, A. O. (1995), pp. 471–472**, on the "equal creation" phrase that Jefferson dropped from the final text; **Boulton (1995), pp. 473–477**, on *Notes on the State of Virginia* and the racial argument. Primary texts: *Summary View* (1774) and the Declaration (1776), both assigned.

### Section 3: 18th-century versus modern meanings (about 250 words)
- Equality of conditions (Tocqueville, as explained by Dr. Ross) versus equality of outcome (common modern usage).
- Fischer's regional orders (Massachusetts, Virginia, Delaware) show that even "egalitarian" societies had stratification. Use this to test the claim of an egalitarian society.
- Armitage's account of Bentham's 1776 review: the Declaration as a claim about foreign policy and natural rights.
- Prompt: What meaning of equality does the 18th century include or exclude that modern usage includes or excludes?
- Source placeholders (confirmed): **Brown, R. D. (2017), pp. 23–24**, on equality of opportunity rather than equal distribution (citing Gordon Wood); **Fischer (1989)**, assigned, for regional stratification in the Massachusetts, Virginia, and Delaware orders (see the Fischer summary for page numbers).

### Section 4: Conclusion (about 75 words)
- Restate your thesis.
- Prompt: What does this comparison suggest about the way Americans use the word "equality" today?
