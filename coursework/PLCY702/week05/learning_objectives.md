## Module 5 Introduction

Many argue the Constitution should be subordinated to the principles of the Declaration of Independence. In this module you will evaluate the Declaration of Independence as having emerged from the tradition of American constitutionalism. You will explore the meaning of equality as it is illuminated by this context.

---

## Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of this module, you will be able to:

1. **Evaluate the Declaration of Independence against similar declarations issued by colonial and local governments.**

2. **Analyze the structure of the Declaration of Independence and assess the function of the concept of "equality" within that structure.**

3. **Identify consistent themes between the Declaration of Independence and other writings by Thomas Jefferson.**

---

## Course Guidance for This Week

**From Prof. Root (Week 5 email):**

- Fischer on the division of classes: can any society function without hierarchy? Elites are needed to organize (for example, defense), but a plebiscite is still a mob. How is oligarchy prevented from forming out of the ranks? Is there a cycle of regimes?
- Crèvecoeur, "What is American?": the toleration of the late 1700s and the idea of a nation held together by an IDEA greater than its members. Are there differences between peoples who come here, and what does that do to the founders' original understanding?
- Jefferson: read the Declaration and the *Summary View* slowly and carefully, noting the anti-slavery passage that was softened in the final text. Compare the charges against the King with current conditions. Prof. Root points to an annotated Declaration at [founding.com](https://founding.com/the-declaration/founding-principles/).
- DeT (Tocqueville): "equality rightly understood" versus equality as the modern world understands it. Does equality of conditions produce hostility toward other groups? Does the passion for equality lead a people toward an aristocracy-in-disguise?
- **Housekeeping:** review the APA *Publication Manual* and keep it at hand. Read slowly and write pithily (see [../slow_reading_lists_fletcher_notes.md](../slow_reading_lists_fletcher_notes.md)).

## Discussion Assignment

**Prompt:** "Describe the egalitarian features of American society noted by its European observers. In what ways did Jefferson's political use of the concept of equality overlap with the kind of social equality these European observers noted, or in what ways did his use of equality differ from or add to theirs? How are these 18th century views of equality consistent or inconsistent with common views of that term today?"
