## Wrapping It Up

Let's begin the week with DeT [de Tocqueville]: There is a lot that is prescient about DeT, but also, there is a lot in DeT that misses the character of the American people. This week, in Chapter 4, he delineated something fundamental and true—the sovereignty of the people. You should read this, and think about today. How sovereign are the people today? It is certainly true that the people are the "foundation of [all] human institutions" here (I use Mansfield's translation of De T here, as should you for any serious study of his work). By chapter 5 De T makes a pretty irresponsible comment, that, we cannot examine the Union without studying the "state." But what about the Federalist Papers? If you have read all the papers, Publius takes us through a nice long ride through the European institutions, and finds them ALL wanting. This is where DeT seems to fail in understanding truly, the American Character. This is astonishing when he writes a little later that people obey society because it is merely "useful" to him. What?! There is a big difference between useful/interests, and principles of liberty. DeT downplays the latter in many respects throughout his long book. However, he is a keen observer, when he notes in same chapter, that America LACKS administration. How on earth did we ever become a power lacking experts to guide us? This lack of administration is a telling observation by DeT. Think on this much. And you should pay careful attention to his parts on Centralization/administrative centralization. It is this part, and his correct observations, we find ourselves in today. The principles of politics are the same no matter the country—centralized administration = tyranny and conformity.

So I return to how I began in this post: are the people really sovereign today? OR is our consent being taken from us—are we being disenfranchised? For example: in 2020 one party's voters were denied the vote, and in the present, one party wants non-citizens to vote—these are all disenfranchisement practices, that steal the consent of the voters.

Think on this much.

## Key Takeaways

**Core Question:** How sovereign are the people today?

**Critical Tensions:**
1. **De Tocqueville's Prescience vs. Limitations:** While DeT correctly identifies American sovereignty of the people as fundamental, he misses key aspects of American character and downplays principles of liberty in favor of utility/interest-based explanations
2. **American Exceptionalism:** Publius (Federalist Papers) demonstrates American uniqueness by rejecting ALL European institutional models, whereas DeT relies on European comparison framework
3. **Administration vs. Liberty:** America's historical LACK of administration was not a weakness but a feature—yet modern administrative centralization threatens this founding principle
4. **Centralization as Root of Tyranny:** "Centralized administration = tyranny and conformity" (principle constant across all political systems)

**Modern Application Questions:**
- People's sovereignty in contemporary context: erosion of voter consent through disenfranchisement practices (denying votes, extending votes to non-citizens)
- Role of administrative state in undermining constitutional foundations
- How to preserve principles of liberty vs. utilitarian governance

## Study Notes

**Primary Source Guidance:**
- Use Mansfield's translation of *Democracy in America* for serious scholarly work
- Read Tocqueville Chapters 4–5 carefully for sovereignty discussion
- Engage with Federalist Papers as counterpoint to DeT's European-centric analysis
- Note De Tocqueville's observations on American lack of professional administration

**Discussion Framework:**
This wrap-up invites reflection on the tension between:
- **De Tocqueville's thesis:** People as foundation of institutions, utility-based obedience
- **American founding principle:** Sovereignty rooted in principles of liberty, not mere utility
- **Contemporary reality:** Degree to which people retain actual sovereignty vs. formal sovereignty

**Capstone/Research Connection:**
Prof. Root's emphasis on people's sovereignty, consent, and disenfranchisement practices may inform research on:
- Policy implementation legitimacy (do affected communities retain meaningful consent?)
- Administrative centralization and citizen autonomy
- Digital infrastructure governance: does centralized health IT mandate preserve or erode popular sovereignty in policy decisions?
