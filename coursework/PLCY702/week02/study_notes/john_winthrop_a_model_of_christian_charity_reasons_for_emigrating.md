# John Winthrop - A Model of Christian Charity & Reasons for Emigrating

Document 6: John Winthrop, A Model of Christian Charity & Reasons for Emigrating

Executive Summary John Winthrop’s writings outline the uncompromising theological and social mission of the Massachusetts Bay Colony. In Reasons for Emigrating, he argues that the churches of Europe have become irredeemably corrupt, necessitating a purified sanctuary in the New World. In his famous lay sermon, A Model of Christian Charity, Winthrop establishes a strict covenantal framework. He warns that the colony's survival depends entirely on mutual charity, strict social cohesion, and absolute obedience to God's laws; individualism is fiercely rejected in favor of the collective spiritual mission.

Student Notes & Key Takeaways

Escape from Corruption, Not Pluralism: Winthrop clarifies that the Puritans did not cross the Atlantic to establish modern, pluralistic religious liberty or toleration. They sought to escape the decadence of the Church of England to establish a pure, unified, and strictly regulated church-state where dissenting views were often expelled (Winthrop, Reasons for Emigrating, pp. 309–311).

"City upon a Hill": Often misunderstood today as a boastful beacon of democratic freedom, Winthrop intended this phrase as a high-stakes, terrifying warning. The eyes of the world were on them; if they broke their covenant through selfishness or greed, they would become a cautionary tale and bring eternal shame to God's reputation (Winthrop, A Model of Christian Charity).

Radical Communal Obligation: Winthrop emphasized extreme social solidarity ("we must bear one another's burdens"). He argued that God ordained inequality (rich and poor) so that men would need each other. Individual economic ambition and hoarding were considered sins; personal desires had to be completely subordinated to the survival, purity, and welfare of the total community (Winthrop, A Model of Christian Charity).
