# Dissertation Case Studies — Real-World Policy Evidence Tracker

## Purpose
This folder tracks **real-world, current-events policy developments** relevant to the dissertation topic (healthcare digital infrastructure as a driver of public policy outcomes in emerging economies) as they happen — distinct from the formal, peer-reviewed academic literature review in [../literature_review/](../literature_review/).

Unlike the literature review (which assesses published academic research), this folder captures **live natural experiments and policy actions** — government mandates, interoperability pacts, national reform rollouts — that can later serve as:
1. **Case study evidence** for the dissertation's empirical chapters (e.g., tracking Mexico's 2026–2027 digital health mandate as it unfolds).
2. **Theoretical framework material** — applying course concepts (e.g., Stone's *Policy Paradox* carrot/stick inducements) to real, current policy actions.
3. **Comparative case material** — contrasting countries at different digital health maturity stages (e.g., Mexico's compulsory mandate vs. Guatemala's earlier voluntary pathway).

## Files
- [mexico_digital_health_mandate_2026.md](mexico_digital_health_mandate_2026.md) — Mexico's January 2026 General Health Law reform mandating digital health records/interoperability, the August 2026 IMSS–Ministry of Health pact, and the January 2027 Universal Health Service phased rollout. Includes a gap analysis between the legal mandate and real-world implementation capacity.
- [guatemala_digital_health_transformation.md](guatemala_digital_health_transformation.md) — Guatemala's earlier-stage, PAHO-aligned digital health transformation pathway (as of October 2025), used as a comparative case to Mexico's compulsory approach.
- [policy_instruments_carrot_stick_framework.md](policy_instruments_carrot_stick_framework.md) — A theoretical framework note applying Stone's (2012) *Policy Paradox* Ch. 11 (Inducements) carrot/stick taxonomy to DPI-fostering policy instruments, grounded in the Mexico/Guatemala/U.S. agricultural subsidy examples.

## Working Principle
**Governments do not build healthcare technology — they wield policy instruments (mandates, subsidies, certification standards, interoperability pacts) that create the conditions under which technology adoption becomes possible, required, or rewarded.** This reframes the dissertation's core question: it's not just "does digital infrastructure reduce reactive spending?" but "which policy instrument, under which capability conditions, successfully drives that infrastructure into place?"
