## What Is a D.P.P.? (vs. a Ph.D.)
The Doctor of Public Policy (D.P.P.) is a **professional/applied doctoral degree**, distinct from a research-focused Ph.D.:
- **Ph.D.:** Typically research-focused; the capstone output is a *dissertation* — an original theoretical/methodological contribution to the discipline's scholarly body of knowledge, evaluated by a formal dissertation defense.
- **D.P.P.:** Emphasizes practical application — equips graduates to address complex policy issues, influence public decision-making, and pursue leadership in government, nonprofit, or private-sector settings. The culminating project is a **Capstone Project** (not a dissertation), built around applying rigorous scholarly methods to a real-world public policy challenge, assessed through the Capstone I–IV course sequence (PLCY 887–890) per the Helms School of Government Capstone Handbook.
- Liberty's D.P.P. is explicitly grounded in the American constitutional tradition, Christian worldview, and the theological/philosophical roots of American government, alongside policy analysis, research design, and research methods training.

**Why this fits this project well:** the stated career goals (Foreign Service Officer, Chief Data Officer, applied healthcare-policy modernization in emerging economies) were always oriented toward applied practitioner impact rather than an academic/faculty research career — the Capstone format is arguably a better structural fit for that goal than a traditional dissertation would have been. The existing topic, research question, and theoretical framework (see [capstone/topic_overview.md](../capstone/topic_overview.md)) remain valid; what differs is the final deliverable's framing (problem → analysis → actionable policy recommendations, rather than a 5-chapter dissertation) and the course sequence/terminology below.

## Program Summary
- **Degree:** Doctor of Public Policy (D.P.P.), Economic Policy Cognate
- **Total Credits:** 60 c.h.
- **Structure:** Program Courses (18 c.h.) → Research Methods & Tools (15 c.h.) → Economic Policy Cognate (9 c.h.) → Capstone Courses (18 c.h.)
- **Continuous enrollment** required once in capstone phase (Fall/Spring/Summer)
- **Time limit:** 5 years from admission

## Graduation Requirements
- 60 credit hours completed
- Max 50% transfer credit (with approval)
- 3.0 GPA minimum; no grade below B- counts
- Successful completion of Research Concept (PLCY 885)
- Successful completion of the Capstone sequence (PLCY 887 Capstone I, 888 Capstone II, 889 Capstone III, 890 Capstone IV), per the Helms School of Government Capstone Handbook
- Degree Completion Application filed in final semester
